A **Boltzmann brain** is portable, verifiable, model-agnostic knowledge. It is defined by a protocol, not by
vitruvio. The protocol is the paper *Boltzmann Brain: A Versioned, Distributable, and Model-Agnostic Knowledge
Architecture* (Alex Fiorenza, Gaussia) —
[read the PDF](https://github.com/gaussia-labs/papers/blob/main/papers/2026-07-boltzmann-brain/boltzmann_brain_en.pdf)
— and [`pyboltzmann`](https://github.com/gaussia-labs/pyboltzmann) implements it. Vitruvio is the engine the
protocol deliberately leaves out: the indices, the query planner, the embeddings, the ingestion gate and the
distribution tooling.

The paper rests on one principle. Knowledge produced with language models tends to get locked inside one provider,
one agent or one vector store, where it is hard to audit, reindex, version or move. So **the brain conserves,
validates and retrieves knowledge, and an external, interchangeable model processes, contextualises and uses it.**
The brain embeds no model of its own.

## How it works, in plain words

1. **Evidence goes in as it is.** You register a document, a transcript, an image. It is stored byte for byte in
   *canonical* memory. Registering it does not claim it is true — only that this evidence was incorporated and
   preserved.
2. **Everything is a block.** Each unit of knowledge is a typed block identified by the SHA-256 of its canonical
   serialisation. Same content, same identity, on any machine — which is what makes two brains comparable and
   deduplication automatic.
3. **Blocks live in five modules**, each with its own Merkle DAG and one root that commits to exactly which
   blocks it holds. A root proves a module's integrity without shipping its internals.
4. **Interpretation is separate and cited.** A fact, an event or a procedure derived from evidence is another
   block, and a *provenance* record ties it to the evidence and to whoever derived it. When models improve, you
   re-derive and supersede — and the record of what was previously believed survives.
5. **A brain is versioned and distributable.** A snapshot commits the roots; history is a chain of snapshots,
   and a graph once two people's work is reconciled. The whole thing is an OCI image layout, so any registry can
   hold it, and a consumer can install one module and verify it alone.
6. **A search returns evidence, never prose.** The result is a bundle of blocks with their identities and
   provenance. There is no answer field and there will not be one: synthesising the answer is the model's job,
   outside the brain, and its citations have to point at blocks the brain actually returned.

## The layout on disk

```
demo/
├── oci-layout  index.json  blobs/sha256/...   # the layout: the SDK's, and portable
├── boltzmann/                                 # compositions, snapshots, the head pointer
└── .vitruvio/                                 # derived state. Deleting it costs time, never knowledge.
    ├── indices/  stats/  embeddings/
    └── calibration.json          # only if you wrote one; nothing generates it yet
vitruvio.toml                                  # beside the brain, and committed
```

Vitruvio adds exactly one file and one directory, both outside the layout, so `oras cp` and every other OCI tool keep
working on a brain vitruvio has touched.

## Five modules, and why the count is fixed

| module | holds | removal |
|---|---|---|
| canonical | the evidence itself, as registered, plus deterministic normalized views | drop (policy-gated) |
| episodic | what happened, when | **supersede only** — append-only by protocol |
| semantic | facts, definitions, claims | drop |
| procedural | goals and ordered steps | drop |
| provenance | who derived what from what, and everything that was removed | written by the protocol |

Each module has its **own Merkle DAG and its own root**. That is what makes a selective install coherent: you can
take semantic memory and leave a canonical layer of gigabytes behind, and what you took still verifies on its own.

## Registration is not assertion

Registering a source does **not** say it is true. Canonical memory asserts one thing: *this evidence was incorporated
and preserved*. Every interpretation of it is a separate block that cites it through provenance.

That separation is what makes a brain re-interpretable. When the models improve, you re-derive the interpretations
against the same evidence and supersede the old ones — and the record of what was previously believed survives.

## Three hash levels, deliberately not one type

- **BlockId** — the identity of one block, over its canonical bytes.
- **MerkleRoot** — the identity of a module's whole composition.
- **OciDigest** — the identity of a blob or manifest in the layout.

They are distinct types throughout, in the SDK and in vitruvio, and the type checker enforces it. Collapsing them
into "a hash" is how a client comes to compare a block id against a root and conclude something false.
